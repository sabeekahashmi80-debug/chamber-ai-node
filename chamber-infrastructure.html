Chamber infrastructure: a bad seal shouldn't read as a bad part

A test result is only as good as the chamber running it. Ignis continuously watches the enclosure itself — seals, gaskets, airflow, and atmosphere — so a chamber fault never gets mistaken for a product failure, and never quietly invalidates a result nobody caught.

What it watches for

Continuous, automated monitoring of the testing envelope itself, not just the part inside it.

01

Gasket & seal leak tracking

Watches the chamber door boundary for the microscopic hot or cold air plumes that indicate a seal is degrading — before it drifts the chamber out of tolerance.

02

Condensation & frost detection

Flags localized moisture pooling or early frost formation on chamber walls, often visible well before internal humidity sensors register a shift.

03

Airflow & heating uniformity

Maps blocked vents or a failing heating element bank inside the chamber, so temperature uniformity across the test volume stays where it's supposed to be.

04

One record, not two stories

Chamber-side and product-side data share the same timestamp, so if a result looks off, you can tell immediately whether it was the part or the chamber.

Why this matters for compliance

Standards like IATF 16949, AS9100, and DO-160 depend on chambers holding a validated envelope throughout a run. A seal degrading mid-test, undetected, is exactly the kind of gap that turns up in an audit. Continuous chamber-side monitoring is designed to close that gap — see how the fusion engine keeps it all on one timeline.
