Why point-of-capture fusion, and why general AI doesn't solve this

The technical case for a chamber-specific logger, in plain terms: what "fusion" actually means, why it has to happen at the edge, and why AI built for factories doesn't transfer to environmental chambers.

The problem with today's stack

A conventional setup pairs a hardware logger — reading temperature, humidity, and other channels — with a separate thermal or IR camera and its own software. Each writes its own file, on its own clock.

TIME DRIFT

Matching timestamps by hand

A CSV export from a logger and a video file from a separate thermal camera rarely land on the same clock. Someone has to line them up manually before the data means anything — and small drift compounds over a long cycle.

TOOL STACK

Multiple licenses, one answer

Logger software, thermal analysis software, and whatever pulls it together for a report — each with its own license, its own learning curve, and its own place for something to go wrong.

What point-of-capture fusion means

Instead of two tools writing two files, Ignis captures environmental sensor data and thermal/infrared imagery through one unit, tied to a single timestamp at the moment of capture — not reconciled afterward. That single synchronized stream is what the on-device AI actually analyzes, which is what makes real-time flagging possible instead of after-the-fact review.

Why general factory AI doesn't transfer

This isn't a case of nobody having built AI-plus-thermal before — it's that what exists is built for a different problem.
