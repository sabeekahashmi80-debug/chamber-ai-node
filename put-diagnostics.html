PUT diagnostics: catch the failure before the part does

Stop catching board failures after the test cycle already ruined the prototype. Ignis watches the product under test — PCBAs and electronic sub-assemblies — through the full thermal shock and vibration profile, and flags the early signs, not just the wreckage.

What it watches for

Built specifically for automotive PCBA and electronic sub-assemblies going through hot, cold, and vibration cycles — not general factory-floor monitoring repurposed for a chamber.

01

Micro-thermal runaway prediction

Tracks subtle temperature deltas on individual components — MCUs, MOSFETs, capacitors — to flag early degradation before it cascades into a structural short.

02

Solder joint stress monitoring

Watches for microscopic fractures and thermal impedance shifts across BGA and surface-mount joints under vibration or rapid temperature cycling.

03

Fault code mapping

Where the board streams live telemetry — CAN bus or Automotive Ethernet — Ignis can align a physical hotspot in time with the diagnostic fault code it produces, so an engineer sees cause and effect together instead of two separate logs to reconcile by hand.

04

Early flagging, not just post-mortem

Anomalies surface while the cycle is still running, so a test can be paused or adjusted before a prototype is destroyed rather than after.

Why this is a different problem than factory-floor AI

General predictive-maintenance AI is trained to watch a motor or a weld run continuously in steady conditions. A PCBA under test is deliberately pushed through extreme, rapidly-changing temperatures on a short cycle — the failure signatures and the noise look completely different, which is why chamber-specific detection matters.
